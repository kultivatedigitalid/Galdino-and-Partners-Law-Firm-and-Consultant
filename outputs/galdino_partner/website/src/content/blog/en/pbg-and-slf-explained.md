Leasing retail premises or a warehouse often starts with price, space and access. Building legality and approved use belong in the same decision. A tenant company’s NIB does not replace building records.

## Two documents, two purposes

PBG concerns building approval against technical standards. SLF concerns a building’s functional fitness for use. Both need to be read against the condition, intended use and applicable building requirements.

The starting question is not simply whether a permit exists, but whether the available records match the building and proposed activity.

## When a review is useful

Review requirements when constructing, renovating, changing use, taking over a facility or preparing operations. A building previously used for a different activity may need further assessment.

Older IMB records, where available, should be considered alongside current conditions and requirements. Do not assume every older document is automatically invalid or automatically sufficient.

## Check before signing a lease

Request the owner’s document list, available drawings, floor area and storey data, approved use and records of physical changes. Match these against the tenant’s proposed operations.

Agree who supplies records, pays for assessments, handles remedial work and coordinates with authorities. A lease that leaves these responsibilities unclear can complicate follow-up.

## Separate administration from technical work

Application coordination is different from preparing drawings, assessing structures, testing systems or carrying out repairs. Appropriate technical professionals are needed where requirements call for specialist assessment.

Ask quotations to separate assistance, drawings, studies, tests, levies and physical work. Clear scope is more useful than an unexplained single price.

## Plan operating decisions

Create a checklist linking each gap to an owner and target date. Do not set an opening date solely from an administrative estimate. Building condition reviews and authority decisions may affect the schedule.

Use an initial consultation to map available records, unresolved issues and technical support needs before commissioning further work.
