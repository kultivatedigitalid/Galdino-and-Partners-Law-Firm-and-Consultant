Choosing a KBLI code is more than matching a term to your business name. Start with the work actually performed: what is sold, how products or services are created, and who receives them. This makes a licensing discussion more concrete.

## Start with the activity flow

Describe the steps from buying materials or accepting an assignment to delivering the result. Distinguish manufacturing, trading another party’s goods, installation and maintenance. These roles may need different classification reviews even within one company.

For example, a business might sell hardware and develop custom software. Calling it a “technology company” is not enough. Record revenue sources and the team’s actual work rather than relying on marketing language.

## Read the scope and exclusions

KBLI is the economic activity classification published by BPS. The KBLI 2025 publication provides a reference for understanding activity scope. Read beyond a code’s title: check its description, exclusions and relationship with other activities.

If your activity is difficult to classify, retain two or three candidates with reasons. Open questions can be discussed during assessment without immediately adding numerous unused codes.

## Align company records

Review the company’s stated purposes, OSS activity profile, locations, scale and capacity. Administrative consistency alone is insufficient if actual operations differ from the information supplied. For a growing business, distinguish existing activities from future plans.

A classification update should not automatically be equated with a requirement to obtain every licence again. BPS explains that implementing KBLI 2025 does not require businesses to obtain new licences. Specific adjustments still depend on the records and systems concerned.

## Prepare a practical worksheet

- Actual activities and revenue sources.
- Products or services delivered.
- The company’s role in the business chain.
- Location and capacity for each activity.
- Candidate codes and unresolved questions.
- Available corporate documents and NIB.

This worksheet does not replace an assessment; it helps everyone start with the same facts.

## After selecting classifications

Review the risk level and requirements attached to the activity in the applicable system. Identify who prepares evidence, when it is needed and whether supporting permits apply. A useful classification discussion ends with actions, not merely a code number.
