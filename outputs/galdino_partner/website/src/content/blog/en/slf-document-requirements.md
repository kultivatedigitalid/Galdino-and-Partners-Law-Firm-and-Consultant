SLF preparation starts with explaining the existing building: its approvals, what was actually built and how functional fitness and safety have been assessed. Adapt the requirements instead of copying another project's checklist.

## Consolidate administrative records

Prepare owner or company identity, land-control information and available PBG or IMB records. Reconcile addresses, names, areas and uses. Record the history of any ownership or building changes.

Administrative records support traceability but do not independently establish technical fitness. Both evidence groups need to be managed together.

## Review as-built drawings

As-built drawings describe actual completed conditions. Differences from the design should be recorded for technical review. Utility archives, installation changes and maintenance records help explain facility condition.

## Prepare inspection evidence

The inspection type and assessor depend on the building and applicable requirements. Record findings with corrective actions, task owners and completion evidence. Complete forms are not a substitute for site inspection.

Certificates for particular installations, where relevant, must be understood within their scope. Electrical SLO and SLF do not replace one another.

## Plan follow-up

Separate archive collection, inspection, remediation and authority processing. Required repairs can affect the occupancy plan. Start early so operations teams have time to resolve findings.

A useful initial assessment produces a clear requirements list, not a promise that every building can complete the process in the same period.
