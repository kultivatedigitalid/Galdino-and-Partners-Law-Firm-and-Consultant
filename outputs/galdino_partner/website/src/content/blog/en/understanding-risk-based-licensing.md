Risk-based licensing connects a business activity with the level of potential harm it creates. The process should therefore begin by understanding the activity and its classification—not by selecting a licence in isolation.

## Three points to map

### 1. Business activities and classification

A company may conduct several activities. Each requires an appropriate classification, while its articles, OSS records, and actual operations should remain aligned.

### 2. Risk level

The assessed risk determines the legal instrument required. A business identification number may be central in one context, while standard certification or additional licences may apply in another.

### 3. Sector-specific rules

OSS is not the only source of obligations. Location, products, employment, environmental matters, buildings, and technical authorities may introduce additional requirements.

> Good mapping reduces the risk of repeating documents and changing structures midway through the process.

## Practical first steps

- List the activities actually conducted.
- Match business language to the official classification.
- Identify the location and scale of each activity.
- Record licences, certifications, and continuing obligations.
- Seek a review where structures or activities depend on one another.

This article provides general information. The final pathway must be assessed against the company’s circumstances and the rules in effect at the time of application.
