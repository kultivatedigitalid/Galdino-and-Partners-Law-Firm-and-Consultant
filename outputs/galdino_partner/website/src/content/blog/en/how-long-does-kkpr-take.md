No single duration fits every KKPR requirement. Before setting a target, separate applicant preparation, pathway selection, review and corrective follow-up.

## Establish the correct pathway

Spatial information, activity and application conditions affect the confirmation or approval route. Project status and existing records also matter. Relying only on another site's experience can produce an unsuitable target.

## Review location-data readiness

Coordinates, polygons, area, land-control evidence and activity plans need to agree. Discrepancies may require clarification before the next stage. Record the map source and who prepared the information.

A shared information-gap list distinguishes immediately actionable work from tasks awaiting the owner or technical professionals.

## Separate working time from authority time

A consultant's estimate generally describes work within its control after complete information is received. Authority review, correction requests and decisions can add time. The quotation should state when the estimate starts and what it excludes.

If a relevant service-time standard exists, read its completeness conditions and procedural requirements. It is not automatically the total time from the first consultation.

## Schedule around dependencies

Connect KKPR requirements with land transactions, design, environment and building approvals. Establish decision points before committing to difficult-to-change operational dates.

Bring an activity and location summary to the initial consultation. A useful schedule states assumptions, task owners and conditions that could change the estimate.
