Building owners may hold IMB records while current discussions use the term PBG. The useful question is whether the archive and present conditions still describe the same building.

## Start with available records

Collect decisions, drawing attachments, relevant payment evidence and change records. Note the issuing authority, reference, location, use, area and owner details. Check that attachments are legible and belong to the same approval.

Incomplete archives should be traced through the relevant authority. A separate drawing copy should not be assumed to constitute complete evidence of an approval.

## Compare the actual building

Check whether floor count, area, use, layout or utilities have changed. Small renovations and major alterations cannot be assessed solely from the name of the work. A change register helps technical professionals determine the review needed.

## Identify today's requirement

New development, renovation, expansion and occupancy preparation can have different needs. Review older records alongside current conditions and applicable rules. Avoid assuming that every IMB is automatically invalid or automatically covers later alterations.

Discuss the appropriate process with the responsible authority. Consistent information makes questions more precise and reduces repeated review.

## Preserve the change history

Maintain drawing versions, completed works, change dates and responsible parties. These records support PBG preparation, functional-fitness assessment, transactions and ongoing facility management.
