Expansion is often treated as a commercial project, but a change in activity, location, product, or capacity may carry legal consequences. An early review helps prevent gaps between operational plans and corporate records.

## Areas to review

1. **Corporate documents:** do the purpose, objectives, and management records remain accurate?
2. **Business classification:** is the new activity covered by an appropriate classification?
3. **Location:** do zoning, building, and environmental approvals support the activity?
4. **Contracts:** do leases, cooperation agreements, and procurement documents allocate responsibility clearly?
5. **Employment:** does additional hiring create new obligations?
6. **Sector licences:** does the product or model fall under a technical authority?

## Build one dependency list

Some processes cannot run in parallel. Record relationships between documents, task owners, target dates, and external parties that need to respond. This becomes a practical basis for internal communication and periodic review.

The checklist must be adapted to the specific expansion. It does not replace a legal review of current documents and facts.
